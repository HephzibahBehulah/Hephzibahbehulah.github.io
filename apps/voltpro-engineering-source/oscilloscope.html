VoltPro Oscilloscope — User Guide

A two-channel sampled-data instrument for simulation and imported captures. Start with the demonstration, then change one setting at a time. This browser does not directly measure external voltages.

1. Quick start: verify the demonstration

  1. Choose Project → 50 Hz demonstration or Auto Setup. This resets the instrument to its reproducible starting settings; save your current settings first if needed.
  2. CH1 is a yellow 50 Hz sine, 4 Vpp, zero offset. CH2 is a cyan 50 Hz square, 2 Vpp, zero offset and 50% duty. Both channels use 1 V/div; their zero references are vertically separated.
  3. The timebase is 5 ms/div: ten divisions show 50 ms, or 2.5 cycles of a 50 Hz signal. Trigger is CH1, rising edge, 0 V, with 50% pre-trigger.
  4. The simulated capture has 5,000 samples at 100 kSa/s. CH1 should read approximately −2 V to +2 V, 4 Vpp, 1.414 Vrms and a 20 ms period. CH2 should read approximately −1 V to +1 V, 2 Vpp and 50% duty.
  5. At default settings the automated checks allow 0.012 V Vpp error, 0.005 V CH1 RMS error, 0.02 Hz frequency error and 0.1 percentage point duty error. Sampling and quantization cause small differences.
  6. Click Run for repeated acquisitions, Stop to freeze the captured data, or Single for one valid triggered capture.

2. Workspace and menus

The waveform viewport dominates the workspace. Its graticule has 10 horizontal × 8 vertical major divisions, with subdivisions and stronger centre axes. CH1 and CH2 use names and reference markers as well as colour. The upper marker identifies trigger time; the amber edge marker and dashed line identify trigger level.

The top has one menu row. File handles imports and exports; View handles zoom, cursors and the control dock; Insert/Tools opens the generator; Project loads the demonstration; Simulation controls acquisition; Reports exports captures; Help opens this guide. Dropdowns open over the waveform and close when the pointer leaves. Escape also closes them.

The right dock contains Vertical, Horizontal, Trigger, Acquisition, Generator and Display groups. Scroll inside the dock to reach lower controls. Its header button hides/reopens the controls without resizing the waveform viewport. Measurements sit above the single bottom navigation bar. Bottom links switch projects; Oscilloscope is the current project.

3. Signal sources and generator

Simulation generates deterministic sampled signals. The Sources rail button or Insert/Tools → Signal generator opens independent CH1/CH2 generators. Supported shapes are sine, square, triangle, sawtooth, pulse and DC.

SettingMeaning
FrequencyCycles per second (Hz), 0.01–100,000 Hz. Not applicable to DC.
AmplitudePeak-to-peak volts, 0–40 Vpp; a 4 Vpp centred sine has a 2 V peak.
OffsetDC voltage added to the waveform, −20 to +20 V. This is the signal value for DC sources.
PhaseWaveform phase in degrees, −360 to +360.
DutyHigh-time percentage for square/pulse signals, 1–99%. Other shapes disable this control.
Noise peakOptional deterministic perturbation, 0–5 V. Reproducible noise can affect threshold measurements.

Parameters that do not apply to the selected shape are disabled. Generator changes affect the next acquisition; they do not rewrite a stopped capture.

CSV imports a frozen two-channel record. Run and Single are disabled for imported data; switch to Simulation or Auto Setup to generate a new capture. Hardware remains disabled: no supported adapter is installed. The developer adapter interface requires actual device sample rate, record length and bandwidth; browser software alone cannot acquire external voltages.

4. Acquisition, sampling and honest limits

Simulation sampling is separate from screen refresh. Sample rate is 100–1,000,000 Sa/s; record length is 100–100,000 samples. Record duration is approximately samples ÷ sample rate. Changing V/div or time/div changes the view, not the acquired samples.

The model uses a ±10 V input range and selectable 8/12/16-bit, zero-preserving symmetric quantization. Samples at the input rail produce a possible-clipping warning. Reduce source amplitude/offset when needed; changing V/div alone cannot repair clipped acquired data.

The simulated analog bandwidth is ideal/unlimited, with no analog anti-alias filter. Ideal square/pulse/sawtooth edges contain harmonics beyond Nyquist and are warned. Aim for at least 20 samples per requested cycle for this instrument's measurements. Raising sample rate can help, but also shortens a fixed-length record; increase record length when you need more cycles.

Acquisition values in the identification block describe the captured record. Controls can contain settings for the next acquisition. Stop freezes samples; display scaling, colours, cursors and panning remain usable.

5. Vertical controls

  • Enabled shows/hides a channel trace. Captured values may still appear in the measurement strip with a hidden label.
  • V/div uses a 1–2–5 sequence. Smaller values enlarge the trace. It does not change the signal's measured amplitude.
  • Position shifts that channel's zero reference in divisions. Moving a trace is not adding DC offset.
  • DC coupling preserves AC and DC components.
  • AC coupling uses a first-order 10 Hz high-pass response and 150 ms warm-up. It removes steady offset, attenuates low frequencies and can distort pulses. It is not an ideal DC subtraction.
  • GND captures zero reference. If used on the trigger source, a Normal or Single acquisition can wait indefinitely because there is no edge.
  • Colour changes trace appearance; channel names remain visible for accessibility.

Coupling changes apply on acquisition. A stopped trace keeps its previous data; the scale label distinguishes captured coupling from a different pending coupling setting.

6. Horizontal scale, zoom and pan

Time/div is shared by both channels and follows a 1–2–5 sequence. Ten times the setting is the visible time span. Horizontal position is in seconds relative to trigger time, which is 0 on the axis.

  • Wheel: zoom timebase in/out. Shift+wheel: pan time.
  • With the canvas focused, Left/Right arrows pan; +/= zooms in; − zooms out.
  • Drag an empty part of the plot to pan. Double-click or View → Reset view restores 5 ms/div and zero horizontal position.
  • View → Zoom in/out provides menu alternatives.

Zooming out cannot invent data beyond the captured record. If the trace ends before the viewport edge, acquire a longer record or return to a smaller time span. Auto Setup resets the whole instrument, whereas Reset view only changes the timebase/position.

7. Triggering and acquisition states

Select CH1/CH2, rising/falling slope and a voltage level within the signal's range. Enter a numeric level or drag the amber level line near its marker. Trigger time anchors both channels to the same event.

Mode/actionBehaviour
AutoUses valid edges when found. After 500 ms without one, captures an untriggered record so the display can update.
NormalWaits for a valid edge and retains the last capture while waiting. An unreachable level does not replace the record.
SingleArms, waits for one valid edge, captures once, then stops. It does not force an untriggered timeout capture.
Pre-trigger fraction0–0.9 of the record appears before the trigger; 0.5 means half before and half after.
Holdoff0–10 seconds between eligible triggered acquisitions; useful for rejecting closely spaced events.

Armed means Single is waiting; Waiting means the required edge/holdoff has not been satisfied; Triggered indicates a valid edge; Running repeats acquisition; Stopped freezes the record. A disabled or grounded trigger channel, DC-only signal or out-of-range level can prevent triggering.

Practice: choose Normal, enter a 99 V trigger level and Run. The instrument waits and preserves the old capture. Return the level to 0 V to permit a 50 Hz demo trigger.

8. Measurements and display fidelity

Readouts are channel-specific and use the full captured record, after coupling and quantization—not just the visible zoomed portion. Minimum/maximum are sample extremes; Vpp is max minus min; mean is the arithmetic average; RMS is the square root of the average squared voltage.

Frequency/period average rising crossings of the midpoint between measured minimum and maximum. Duty and pulse width use the same midpoint threshold. Rise/fall use the 10–90% amplitude thresholds and require at least three transition samples. An ideal square edge can therefore correctly show Unavailable for rise/fall rather than a fabricated time.

Too few cycles, no transitions or insufficient sampling produce Unavailable. Hover the affected value for its reason. Short, noisy or clipped records can mislead threshold methods: inspect the trace, capture more cycles and improve sampling before trusting a readout.

Dense records use per-pixel minimum/maximum aggregation to retain narrow captured peaks. Display → Sample-point view reveals sampled locations. Trace halo is adjustable from 0–8 px; it changes appearance, not data. No interpolation can recover detail that was never sampled.

9. Time and voltage cursors

Enable Cursors from the left rail, View/Tools menu or Display group. Drag a dashed cursor line, or enter t1/t2 in seconds and v1/v2 in CH1 volts in Display.

The readout gives ΔV, Δt and 1/Δt. Voltage cursors use the CH1 scale/reference. A zero time separation makes 1/Δt unavailable. Drag near the amber trigger line to change trigger level; drag near a dashed cursor to move that cursor. Empty-plot dragging pans time.

10. CSV, images and saved settings

  1. File → Export captured CSV or Reports → Captured CSV exports all captured samples with timestamps, units and acquisition/settings metadata, even while the view is zoomed.
  2. File → Import waveform CSV accepts UTF-8 comma-separated data with this exact header: time_s,CH1_V,CH2_V. Example:
    time_s,CH1_V,CH2_V
0,0,1
0.00001,0.006283,1
0.00002,0.012566,1
    Use seconds and volts, no empty/non-finite cells, 3–100,000 samples, increasing uniformly spaced timestamps within 0.1%, and files up to 15 MB. Lines starting # are comments/metadata. Invalid imports leave the previous record in place.
  3. PNG or File/Reports → Waveform image downloads the plot with scale/acquisition labels.
  4. Save instrument settings downloads JSON for channel, generator, timebase, trigger, acquisition, display and cursor settings. Restore settings validates that JSON, returns to Simulation and takes a simulated record. Settings files do not contain the waveform samples; export CSV as well when you need to preserve a capture.

Files are browser downloads. Keep both a settings JSON and sample CSV for reproducible work. Imported CSV is a frozen record and is not evidence of connected hardware.

11. Engineering AI assistant

On the engineering-suite page, use the floating AI button at the lower right. Ask it to explain settings, expected values, waveforms and troubleshooting using the measurements you supply. It must not be assumed to read unsaved captures, operate controls or measure physical voltages automatically.

Example: “CH1 is a simulated 50 Hz sine, 4 Vpp, zero offset, 1 V/div and 5 ms/div. Explain the expected RMS and period, then guide me through a rising-edge 0 V trigger.”

12. Troubleshooting, access and limits

SymptomWhat to check
No traceEnable the channel, choose Simulation, use Auto Setup, check position/V/div and whether the view lies outside the captured record.
Waiting/Armed never endsCheck trigger source enabled/coupling, slope, level, DC waveform and holdoff. Normal/Single intentionally wait without a suitable edge.
Display is stationaryA stable triggered periodic signal can look stationary while Running. Check the state; CSV and Stopped captures are intentionally frozen.
Unexpected amplitudeDistinguish Vpp from peak and RMS; inspect AC coupling, offset, quantization and possible ±10 V clipping. V/div is only a display scale.
Unavailable measurementHover for a reason. Capture more cycles/samples, lower frequency or increase sample rate, and inspect signal quality.
CSV rejectedCheck the exact header, comma delimiters, seconds/volts, finite values, sample count, size and uniform increasing timestamps.
Need more canvas spaceHide dock contents using its header button; the reserved dock column remains stable. On small screens scroll the header/dock instead of stacking duplicate bars.

Controls support keyboard focus, numeric entry and visible focus outlines. Tab navigates controls; number-field arrows adjust values; Escape closes menus or this guide. Channel labels complement colour. Reduced-motion preferences disable the armed-state pulse.

No real hardware, FFT, XY, math traces or persistence are currently enabled. Hardware adapters and advanced analysis remain future extensions. The tool is a development/educational instrument, not a calibrated or certified physical measurement device. Sign in when the engineering site's account gate requests it.
